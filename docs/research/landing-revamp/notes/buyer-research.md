# How small private, independent and faith-based schools (about 30–200 students, JK–8) choose school-management software, with Canadian and Ontario specifics

Research date: 2026-10-25. Each source's date is given where the page shows one.

**How to read this.** "Hard evidence" means regulation or policy text, regulator findings, surveys with a stated sample, and review counts or ratings shown on review sites. "Anecdote / vendor claim" means single reviews, vendor marketing, competitor comparisons, blog posts and news commentary. Evidence about **public districts** and **US** schools is flagged as such, with a note on how it might apply here.

**Method limitation.** G2, Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp all returned HTTP 403 to direct fetching, and reddit.com is blocked to the search tool. Review quotes and counts below come from search-engine snippets and summaries of those pages, not from reading the pages directly. Review counts are as the snippet showed them, and short quotes should be re-checked on the live page before they go on the website.

## 1. Who decides and who influences, how long it takes, when schools switch, and budget cycles

### Takeaway
At a small Ontario independent school, the buyer is the operator: an owner/founder or a (often volunteer) board, plus the principal. No Ministry rule constrains them, because the Ministry "does not regulate, licence, accredit or otherwise oversee the day-to-day operation of private schools." The office manager or registrar runs the system day to day. Teachers and parents shape whether it gets adopted, not whether it gets bought. The natural window for an SIS change is the summer between school years, after a 3–5-month implementation that starts in spring. New schools must be ready by the September 1 Notice of Intention deadline.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence (Ontario regulation and policy)
- In Ontario, private schools "operate as businesses or non-profit organizations, independently of the Ministry" and receive "no funding or other financial support from the Ontario government." The Ministry "does not regulate, licence, accredit or otherwise oversee the day-to-day operation of private schools." Operators "set their own policies and procedures" — [Ontario Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual (2013, file dated 2021-11-25)](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- Private-school principals "are not required to have Ontario principal's qualifications," and teachers need not be OCT members. The head may therefore be a founder or owner rather than a career administrator — [Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf); also stated on [ontario.ca Private schools (updated 2026-10-19)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- Every private school must file a Notice of Intention to Operate by **September 1** each year. A school that misses it cannot legally operate that year. The first-year NOI fee is $300. "Non-inspected private schools may only begin operation in September of each [year]" — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf); [ontario.ca NOI page](https://ontario.ca/page/private-schools-notice-intention-operate-private-school)
- A private school must have "5 or more students at or over compulsory school age" — [ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- Market size: Cardus counted "at least 1,445 independent schools" in Ontario serving "over 154,000 students" (2019-20), up almost 52% since 2013-14. "Less than 40% of independent schools are religiously based." Released 2022-11-22 — [Cardus, *Naturally Diverse*](https://old.cardus.ca/news/news-releases/number-of-independent-schools-in-ontario-up-52-since-2013)
- Enrolment grew from 150,831 to 167,802 students between 2021-22 and 2023-24. The report looks at "300+ new independent schools." Founders cite religious orientation and pedagogies such as Montessori, and describe sustainability as a challenge, with success coming from "staff dedication and community involvement." Published 2026-04-28 — [Cardus, *School's Out for the Status Quo*](https://www.cardus.ca/news/news-releases/ontarios-independent-school-sector-resilient-community-oriented-and-growing-robustly-new-study-finds/)
- "Between January 2022 and December 2025, 322 new independent schools have opened and stayed open in Ontario," "the majority of which are small." Among religious-emphasis schools, "a large number are Islamic." Joanna VanHof of Cardus, quoted 2026-07-18 — [CTV/CP24](https://cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/07/18/if-the-gta-population-is-up-why-are-public-school-numbers-down)

#### Anecdote / vendor claims (timing and roles)
- Switch timing: "For an SIS, the cleanest moment is the summer between academic years." Full implementations "typically take three to five months from kick-off to stabilisation." For a start-of-year go-live, start planning 4–5 months earlier. "Most schools time finance switches to the start of the fiscal year." Mid-year switches suit only failing systems, tools that can run in parallel, or tools not tied to the calendar. Faria Education Group, 2026-05-28. This is vendor or consultancy content aimed at international and independent schools — [Faria](https://www.faria.org/insights/switching-software-mid-year-vs-between-cycles-how-to-time-it-right/)
- Veracross (a private-school SIS vendor) says fall is "a popular time to start a new SIS implementation" so that enrolment and re-enrolment can go first — [Veracross fall implementation guide](https://www.veracross.com/resources/fall-implementation-guide/)
- Arbor (a UK MIS vendor) says there is no single best time. For a September go-live it suggests starting after Easter because of staff summer leave — [Arbor](https://arbor-education.com/blog-whats-the-best-time-in-the-year-to-move-your-secondary-to-a-cloud-based-mis/)
- Who evaluates at larger US independent schools: in a Veracross-published case study, Overlake's **technology director** compared Blackbaud and Veracross and asked each vendor for references from schools that had left a legacy system. This is a larger school and vendor-published — [Veracross](https://www.veracross.com/apac-overlake-st-davids-school-sis-best-practices/)
- One budget cycle, from a single **US** private school (Mounds Park Academy): budgeting starts about 10 months before the school year with an enrolment forecast. New tuition rates are published in early February, when re-enrolment opens, and the budget is finalized on the 10th day of school. The Board and its Finance Committee set tuition — [Mounds Park Academy](https://www.moundsparkacademy.org/news/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/20992)
- A competitor's glossary page claims schools often re-evaluate their SIS when a new head of school or technology director inherits an old contract. This is promotional and low-confidence — [OpenEduCat](https://openeducat.org/zh/glossary/what-is-veracross-alternative/)
- Islamic schools (Ontario, 2010): Ontario had "over 50" Islamic schools with "no umbrella organization." A proposed provincial association would pursue shared services and **bulk purchasing**, a sign that decisions are collective and made through networks — [Iqra.ca, Dec 2010](https://www.iqra.ca/2010/ontario-association-of-islamic-schools-takes-off/)

### Inferences
- The SchoolMule site should speak to two readers at once. One is the **principal/owner**: outcomes, cost, risk, parent experience. The other is the **office manager**: migration, daily workflows, report-card production. At a 30–200-student school these may be one or two people. There is rarely an IT director.
- The average Ontario independent school has about 107 students (154,000 / 1,445, from Cardus's 2019-20 figures; my arithmetic). The target band is therefore the middle of the market, not a niche.
- Buying calendar for Ontario: evaluate January–April, while tuition and re-enrolment for next year are being set; sign April–June; migrate July–August; go live in September. **New schools** (300+ since 2022) need a system before their first September, and their NOI deadline is September 1. A "start before September" call to action and a published onboarding timeline fit both groups.
- Because the board or founder often signs, the site should make it easy for the principal to forward a one-page summary (price, security, references) to the board.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of decision rights (owner vs. principal vs. board) at small Canadian independent schools. The role evidence comes from larger US schools or is inferred from Ontario's regulatory structure.
- I found no source on typical sales-cycle length (first contact to signature) for small independent schools. Faria's 3–5 months covers implementation, not the decision.
- Ontario private-school fiscal years are not standardized, and I found no source on them.
- Teachers' formal role in selection, such as sitting on a committee, is undocumented for small schools.

## 2. What buyers fear and object to

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced Canadian fear is **student-data security**. The 2024–25 PowerSchool breach affected about 3.86 million Ontarians, and the 2021 federal finding against Edsby's vendor CoreFour put a Canadian edtech company on record for weak safeguards. Data residency comes second: Canadians strongly prefer domestic hosting in polls, although the polls are old or vendor-sponsored. Other objections are documented mostly through reviews and vendor or anecdotal sources: cost and per-student pricing, add-on fees, parent uptake, teacher training and resistance, a vendor discontinuing its product, and support quality.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence: security and privacy incidents that shape Canadian buyers
- **PowerSchool breach (public boards).** A threat actor used compromised credentials to get into PowerSchool's SIS and support portal between 22 and 28 December 2024 — [MobileIDWorld summary](https://www.mobileidworld.com/ontario-and-alberta-investigation-finds-inadequate-safeguards-in-powerschool-student-data-breach/). In January 2025, 20 Ontario school boards and the Ministry reported the breach to the IPC. About 3.86 million Ontarians were affected. The IPC report (2025-11-17) found the boards, as a group, lacked reasonable safeguards and the "necessary oversight" of PowerSchool — [Hicks Morley summary, 2025-11-20](https://hicksmorley.com/2025/11/20/final-report-released-on-powerschool-cyberattack/); [CP24, 2025-11-18](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2025/11/18/gta-school-boards-did-not-have-reasonable-measures-to-stave-off-student-data-breach-privacy-commissioner)
- The joint Ontario/Alberta findings (2025-11-18) were that some or all bodies:
  - left privacy and security provisions out of their PowerSchool contracts;
  - lacked policies to monitor PowerSchool's safeguards, including remote-support user access and multi-factor authentication;
  - did not limit vendor remote access to specific technical issues;
  - lacked adequate breach response plans.

  The commissioners recommended renegotiating contracts, monitoring vendor safeguards (including a privacy impact assessment), granting remote access only when needed, and keeping breach-response procedures — [OIPC Alberta joint release](https://oipc.ab.ca/ontario-and-alberta-privacy-commissioners-release-investigation-findings-into-powerschool-breach-affecting-school-boards-and-other-educational-bodies/)
- After the breach, the federal Privacy Commissioner said organizations must protect personal information with safeguards suited to its sensitivity, "particularly important when dealing with children's personal information" — [CTV, PowerSchool explainer](https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/what-parents-need-to-know-about-the-powerschool-data-breach/)
- **Edsby / CoreFour.** PIPEDA Findings #2021-002 (2021-05-20): the vendor "had not developed a robust, over-arching information security framework." Problems included weak password requirements for some parent accounts and inadequate protection of student profile-photo thumbnails. The OPC noted the data included grades, absences, learning disabilities and health information. CoreFour agreed to implement all recommendations — [OPC finding](https://priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2021/pipeda-2021-002). In a companion decision, the Ontario IPC found that York Region's board "has not demonstrated that it has reasonable oversight measures" over CoreFour's contractual security obligations — [IPC decision](https://decisions.ipc.on.ca/ipc-cipvp/privacy/en/497566/1/document.do)
- Saskatchewan: the privacy commissioner reported that 32 students were outed as transgender through an Edsby glitch. The commissioner found no evidence of a data-protection agreement between the school divisions and Edsby. I did not verify the article's date — [CTV Saskatoon](https://saskatoon.ctvnews.ca/32-sask-students-outed-as-transgender-due-to-edsby-glitch-privacy-commissioner-1.6709402)
- The 2020 Blackbaud ransomware attack (Blackbaud being a major private-school vendor) reached Canadian institutions. St. Lawrence College in Ontario reported that alumni and donor records were affected. Blackbaud paid a ransom and would not publish a full list of affected organizations. I found no confirmation that Canadian independent K–12 schools were affected — [St. Lawrence College](https://stlawrencecollege.ca/news/important-information-for-alumni-and-donors-blackbaud-data-security-incident); [Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://www.philanthropy.com/news/nonprofits-respond-to-blackbaud-data-breach/)

#### Hard-ish evidence: data residency sentiment
- "78% of [Canadians] refuse to have their data hosted outside of Canada." Léger poll for OVHcloud, 2022. **Vendor-sponsored** — [OVHcloud](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news-survey-canadians-sovereignty-personal-data/)
- In a 2006 OPC/EKOS poll, about two-thirds of Canadians were highly concerned about cross-border transfers of personal information through outsourcing to US companies. **Old** — [Government of Canada news archive, 2006](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2006/06/poll-says-canadians-want-personal-information-treated-more-responsibly.html)

#### Hard evidence: vendor continuity
- Engrade, a free and paid SaaS gradebook acquired by McGraw-Hill, was discontinued. A district reported that in April 2018 McGraw-Hill told it "Engrade will no longer be available after June 30, 2019" — [DSST Public Schools blog](https://blog.dsstpublicschools.org/helix/important-engrade-update); acquisition background: [TechCrunch 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/02/09/mcgraw-hill-buys-engrade-for-50m-as-it-moves-away-from-textbooks-towards-a-future-of-saas). A teacher's blog describes an abrupt Engrade outage near the end of a marking period with "not even a 'fail' message" (undated) — [Free Technology for Teachers](https://freetech4teach.teachermade.com/?p=34637)

#### Anecdote / vendor claims: cost, add-ons, parent uptake, training, support
- **Cost scaling and paywalled features** (Brightwheel reviews, childcare-leaning): cost "grows with the number of parents"; "not all aspects... are accessible without paying a fee"; "a bit pricy." Brightwheel does not publish prices — [Capterra brightwheel reviews](https://capterra.com/p/144060/brightwheel/reviews/?page=3); [G2 brightwheel pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/brightwheel/pricing)
- **Add-on fees**: a Gradelink reviewer wants texting "without an additional fee" — [SoftwareConnect Gradelink reviews](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/gradelink/). Sycamore reviewers praise the "lack of add-on fees" and value for "small or non-profit schools" — [Capterra Sycamore reviews p.2](https://www.capterra.com/p/75048/Sycamore-Education/reviews/?page=2)
- **Affordability as the deciding factor**: a small private elementary school chose SchoolCues mainly for affordability but found its billing interface confusing — [Capterra SchoolCues reviews p.3](https://www.capterra.com/p/206168/SchoolCues/reviews/?page=3)
- **Parent uptake / channel**: in Project Tomorrow Speak Up data (**US**, 2019), 83% of parents prefer personal email for teacher communication, 64% text and 39% a mobile app. Email also leads for general school news (68%) — [EdSurge, 2019-10-15](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-10-15-project-tomorrow-survey-in-an-app-world-email-still-reaches-parents). About a third of parents name word of mouth as their main source of school news, and parents want information "pushed to them" and personalized — [ISM, 2018-01-17](https://isminc.com/advisory/publications/the-source/information-parents-value-most)
- **Parent-app complaints**: Brightwheel reviewers report an app that "never updated when they needed information" and wish it "worked on a computer" — [Capterra brightwheel](https://capterra.com/p/144060/brightwheel/reviews/?page=3). Gradelink parent notifications "caused problems for at least one school in 2026" — [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/gradelink/)
- **Teacher resistance**: resistance to technology adoption remains "a problem among a significant portion of the teacher population," and mandates provoke pushback (2009) — [THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2009/03/01/resistance-is-futile.aspx). Ease of use that "simplifies their jobs" makes buy-in easier — [ASCD](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/six-keys-to-successful-change-management). Toddle reviewers complain that frequent updates are hard to keep up with "while teaching" — [Capterra.ca Toddle](https://www.capterra.ca/software/1029768/toddle)
- **Support and training materials**: Sycamore reviewers found the online manuals thin (older reviews, 2010–2020) — [Capterra Sycamore p.2](https://www.capterra.com/p/75048/Sycamore-Education/reviews/?page=2). A SchoolCues reviewer wrote "The customer support is terrible" — [Capterra SchoolCues](https://capterra.com/p/206168/SchoolCues/reviews/)
- **Small-vendor service as a reason to switch**: an Indian school-software market study lists "dissatisfaction with service from smaller players" among upgrade motivations. **Non-Canadian, low transferability** — [iSPIRT/SoftwareSuggest](https://pn.ispirt.in/tag/school-management-software/)

### Inferences
- After PowerSchool, a principal can now cite a regulator's checklist. The IPC/OIPC findings work as an objection-handling script: contract privacy terms, MFA, limited and logged vendor support access, a breach-response plan, and PIA support. A small vendor that publishes how it meets each item can turn "small vendor = risky" into "small vendor = accountable."
- The PowerSchool breach involved **public boards**. Private-school parents read the same news, and the commissioners' recommendations are the clearest public standard available, so they carry over to private schools.
- The Engrade case shows that even a large owner can discontinue a product. The fear to answer is not "small vendor" as such but "what happens to my data if you stop." A documented export (CSV/PDF of records and report cards) and a data-return clause answer it.
- Parent-adoption objections are best answered with "parents get email; no app needed." The US preference data favours email over apps.

### Gaps
- I found no recent, non-vendor Canadian poll specifically about parents' views on where school data is hosted.
- I found no survey quantifying teacher resistance to SIS rollouts in K–12, and none for small private schools.
- I found no evidence on "lock-in" fears specific to small schools beyond the Engrade case and general guidance.

## 3. What questions buyers ask vendors

### Takeaway
Vendor-published question lists converge on: fit to our processes, total cost (setup, training, maintenance), integrations, training and support responsiveness, ease of use for each role, a demo or trial, security and privacy compliance, reporting, update cadence, accessibility, and references from similar schools. Canadian sources add "Where will it be hosted?" Since the PowerSchool findings, contract and vendor-oversight questions are on the table as well. Parents bring their own questions to schools, such as the records-access policy and police checks, and schools pass them on to vendors.

### Cited Findings

#### Vendor-published lists (anecdote / vendor claims, but they describe how buyers are coached)
- FACTS's 20 questions include:
  - "What are the total costs, including setup, training, and ongoing maintenance?"
  - "How responsive is the support team to queries and issues?"
  - "How intuitive is the user interface for administrators, teachers, students, and parents?"
  - "Is there a demo or trial period...?"
  - "How does the system comply with relevant education and privacy regulations?"
  - "Can you provide references or case studies from other schools...?"
  - "How often does the system receive updates...?"
  - "Is the SIS accessible to all users, including those with disabilities?"

  Undated; © 2026 — [FACTS blog](https://factsmgt.com/blog/key-questions-to-ask-when-selecting-a-student-information-system/)
- Edsembli (Mississauga, Ontario) lists eight questions, including "Where Will It Be Hosted?", "How Easy is It To Use?", "What Sort of Support Does The Vendor Provide?" and "What Are People Saying About The Solution?" Its hosting section discusses cloud vs. on-premise but **does not mention Canadian residency** — [Edsembli](https://edsembli.com/8-key-questions-to-ask-when-choosing-a-new-sis-solution/)
- Blackbaud's K–12 private-school buyer's guide (gated) covers gathering stakeholder input, identifying vendors, "Preparing for a vendor demo," and "Evaluating vendors—including a rating worksheet" — [Blackbaud](https://www.blackbaud.com/?p=251)
- At a US independent school, the evaluator asked each finalist vendor for **references from schools that had moved off a legacy system** — [Veracross case study](https://www.veracross.com/apac-overlake-st-davids-school-sis-best-practices/)

#### Hard evidence: questions created by regulation and regulators
- The Ministry suggests parents ask schools whether they have "a policy on access to student records" and whether they conduct "criminal reference checks on staff." Updated 2026-10-19 — [ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- The PowerSchool recommendations translate into buyer questions about contract privacy and security terms, monitoring of vendor safeguards, MFA, remote-support access, PIAs and breach response — [OIPC Alberta joint release, 2025-11-18](https://oipc.ab.ca/ontario-and-alberta-privacy-commissioners-release-investigation-findings-into-powerschool-breach-affecting-school-boards-and-other-educational-bodies/)
- For schools that transfer records with public boards: before a board principal transfers an original OSR to a private school, a written request and parent (or adult-student) consent are needed. Appendix I is the request form a private school uses to obtain an OSR. The guideline was updated 2025-08-27 — [OSR Guideline s.6.2](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-student-record-osr-guideline); [Appendix I](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-osr-guideline-appendix-i-en-2022-02-04.pdf)

#### Leaders' advice to vendors (US, mixed district/school leaders)
- "Contacting a vendor for a mysterious quote is a waste of time." "Provide an online catalog/ordering process so we can easily get quotes..." "Be clear in promises, pricing, and timelines." "Relationships are worth more than the bottom line. I will buy from vendors I trust." "A couple hundred K-12 leaders," Feb 2025 — [EdWeek WirED Marketer](https://www.edweek.org/advertise/the-wired-marketer-advice-from-k-12-leaders-to-vendors-they-dont-mince-words-2-25-25/2025/03)

### Inferences
Likely questions from a small Ontario principal, ranked (my synthesis of the sources above):
1. What does it cost all-in for my number of students? Are there setup, migration or texting fees?
2. Where is our data stored, who can access it, and what happens if you are breached?
3. Can you import our spreadsheets or old system, and who does it?
4. Will my teachers manage it? How long is training?
5. Will parents use it? Does it work on email only, on a phone, in other languages?
6. Can it produce our report card (provincial-style or our own)?
7. Who else like us uses it, and can I call them?
8. What if you shut down, and can we export everything?

An FAQ in that order is a reasonable starting structure.

### Gaps
- I found no CAIS-, OFIS- or NAIS-published "questions to ask your SIS vendor" guide or RFP template. Their materials appear to be member-gated or don't exist publicly. ISACS and NAIS pages did not surface in searches.
- I found no small-school RFP template in the public domain. Small schools probably don't run formal RFPs, but that is unverified.

## 4. What small-school reviewers praise and complain about (G2, Capterra, Software Advice snippets)

### Takeaway
Across the products, small-school reviewers praise **responsive, human support** and **simplicity / "everything we need in one place."** They complain about **dated or clunky interfaces**, **inflexible report cards**, **glitches**, **billing/payment friction**, **add-on fees**, and **missing parent-communication features** such as translation and phone alerts. Support is the most consistent differentiator, and report cards the most consistent pain point.

## 5. Canadian and Ontario privacy law for Ontario private schools

### Takeaway
Ontario has no general private-sector privacy statute. The Ontario public-sector acts (FIPPA/MFIPPA) cover school boards, not private schools. Whether PIPEDA applies depends on whether the school's activity is "commercial." The OPC's test presumes a school whose core activity is education is **non-commercial** unless it aims to earn **profit for owners**. A charitable, non-profit Islamic or Montessori school is therefore probably outside PIPEDA for core education, and an owner-operated for-profit school probably inside it. The **vendor** itself is subject to PIPEDA for its commercial activity: the OPC investigated Edsby's operator under PIPEDA.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence
- PIPEDA covers "private-sector organizations across Canada that collect, use, or disclose personal information" in commercial activity. Not-for-profit and charity groups are generally not covered unless they engage in "commercial activities that are not central to their mandate." Alberta, BC and Quebec have "substantially similar" private-sector laws. Ontario's substantially similar law covers **personal health information** only. Modified 2024-05-01 — [OPC, PIPEDA in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)
- OPC Interpretation Bulletin, modified 2022-03-24:
  - "Not-for-profit organizations are not automatically exempt from PIPEDA."
  - Tax status "is not determinative."
  - "A private school was found not to be engaged in a commercial activity."
  - The test: "What is the institution's core activity? Is the institution providing educational services as its core activity?" If so, its activities are presumptively non-commercial. The presumption "will be rebutted if the institution has, as one of its objectives, the goal of earning a profit for the owners."
  - A **non-profit daycare** was found to be engaged in commercial activities.

  Source: [OPC Interpretation Bulletin: Commercial Activity](https://priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda-compliance-help/pipeda-interpretation-bulletins/interpretations_03_ca)
- PIPEDA Case Summary #2006-345 concerned a CRA-registered charitable, non-share, non-profit private school whose scholarship committee allegedly disclosed a parent's financial information. The OPC declined jurisdiction: "There was no indication that the school's goal was to earn a profit for its owners" — [OPC 2006-345](https://priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2006/pipeda-2006-345). Commentary notes the school was outside PIPEDA "even though it charged tuition" — [Carters Charity Law Bulletin No. 97 (2006)](https://www.carters.ca/pub/bulletin/charity/2006/chylb97.htm)
- The OPC investigated the edtech vendor CoreFour (Edsby) under PIPEDA. In other words, the vendor's handling of student data was within federal jurisdiction even though its customers were public boards — [PIPEDA Findings #2021-002](https://priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2021/pipeda-2021-002)
- The IPC's FIPPA/MFIPPA jurisdiction covers ministries, agencies, universities, colleges and local institutions such as **school boards**. Private schools are not listed. This is my reading of the IPC material as surfaced in search, not an explicit IPC statement on private schools — [IPC](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/node/3974); [IPC complaints process](https://www.ipc.on.ca/node/2516)
- The OSR Guideline was reviewed against FIPPA and MFIPPA, and notes that MFIPPA "applies to schools operated by school boards" — [OSR Guideline s.1, s.4](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-student-record-osr-guideline)
- BC comparison: independent school authorities are governed by BC's **PIPA** (private-sector law), while FOIPPA applies to "public bodies like boards of education" — [BC Ministry of Education, PIPA page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/program-management/independent-schools/other-legislation-regulations/personal-information-protection-act). BC's Inspector of Independent Schools guidelines (updated July 2021) tie student-record policies to PIPA — [FISA BC copy of guidelines](https://fisabc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/student_record_independent_schools_Updated-July-2021-2.docx)

### Inferences
- The website should not claim "PIPEDA-compliant" as though PIPEDA binds every school. A more accurate framing: "Built to PIPEDA's fair information principles. We handle your data as your service provider, under a written data-processing agreement." This holds whether the school is a non-profit outside PIPEDA or a for-profit inside it.
- Most small Islamic, Christian and Montessori schools are non-profits or charities. Their privacy duty comes mainly from parent trust and contracts rather than statute. That makes a **plain-language privacy promise** and a downloadable DPA more persuasive than legal citations.
- Schools with scholarship or tuition-assistance programs handle parents' financial data, which was the very issue in OPC 2006-345. SchoolMule's QuickBooks-mirrored tuition data is in the same sensitive category.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether the OPC has revisited the 2006 private-school position since 2022, or how Bill C-27 / CPPA (federal privacy reform) stands as of October 2026.
- I did not research Quebec's Law 25 (private-sector act), which applies to Quebec private schools and requires assessments before transferring personal information outside Quebec. It needs a separate pass if Quebec matters.
- The IPC page did not explicitly say "private schools are not covered."

## 6. Ontario Ministry obligations: OSR, OEN, NOI, inspection, OnSIS, report cards, Kindergarten

### Takeaway
A JK–8 Ontario private school is a **non-inspected** school. It does not have to follow the Ontario curriculum. It **may, but is not required to,** use the provincial Elementary Progress / Provincial Report Card. It **may choose** to keep OSRs, and if it does, it must follow the OSR Guideline. Its students cannot receive OENs. Its hard obligations are the annual NOI (September 1) and statistical reporting through OnSIS: aggregate student data and educator data. Report-card format is therefore a matter of school preference, which makes **flexible report-card templates**, and optionally a provincial-style one, a selling point rather than compliance.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence
- "Non-Inspected private schools include **all private elementary schools** as well as any private secondary schools that do not provide courses offering credits toward the OSSD. These schools are not required to follow the Ontario curriculum" — [Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf); confirmed on [ontario.ca Private schools (updated 2026-10-19)](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools)
- "Private schools may, but are not required to, communicate student achievement using either the Elementary Provincial Report Card (for Grades 1-8) or the Provincial Report Card (for Grades 9-12)" — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- A caveat that needs care: under the heading "Additional Policy Requirements Specific to **Inspected** Private Schools Only," the Manual says that under *Growing Success* "the assessment, evaluation and reporting of student achievement in all Ontario schools, including private schools, must be based on the policies and practices in this document." Context places this under inspected schools — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- Inspected (OSSD-credit) schools must have an attendance policy, a procedure for reporting to parents, and OSRs and OSTs kept to Ministry guidelines, plus a central office for secure storage. The Ministry inspects "only those registered private schools that offer credits toward an OSSD," generally every two years, for a fee — [ontario.ca Private schools](https://www.ontario.ca/page/private-schools); [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- OSR Guideline (updated 2025-08-27):
  - "An OSR will be established for each student who enrols in a school operated by a school board or the Ministry of Education."
  - Private schools are listed among schools that "may choose to establish and maintain an OSR," and "All schools that establish and maintain an OSR... must do so in accordance with this guideline."
  - Report cards are retained 5 years after the student leaves; the OSR folder, OST and index card 55 years.
  - Electronic storage is allowed, but report cards "must be filed in the OSR as a hard copy."

  Source: [OSR Guideline](https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-student-record-osr-guideline)
- On closure, "Any Ontario Student Records (OSRs) in the school's possession must be sent to the Ministry (or to another educational institution...)" — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- OnSIS reporting. All private schools must submit statistics (enrolment, staff, courses of study). Non-inspected schools report "aggregate student data once per year, and elemental educator data three times per year." The Manual flags "This policy has been revised," so the current schedule should be confirmed — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- OEN (vendor documentation; confirm with the Ministry):
  - Aggregate data is collected for students "who cannot be issued Ministry Ontario Education Numbers (OENs). This includes all private elementary students and students attending non-inspected private secondary schools."
  - Privately funded students at **inspected** secondary schools do need OENs.
  - Private schools must issue Ministry Educator Numbers (MENs) for their staff through OnSIS.
  - The main count is on October 31.

  Sources: [Edsembli OnSIS private elementary submission](https://manuals.edsembli.com/SIS/Secondary/ReportsS/Ontario/onsisfields/onsisfields2009/privelemsubmissions.htm); [Edsembli OnSIS prerequisites](https://sis.support.edsembli.com/en/support/solutions/articles/8000102258-pre-requisites-for-onsis-all-schools)
- Advertising rule for schools: a private school may not say the Ministry "approved or accredited" its program. Schools without credit authority may not use Ministry course codes "on your school transcripts, report cards, website" — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)
- Kindergarten: the Ministry's *Growing Success – Kindergarten Addendum* (2016) defines the Kindergarten Communication of Learning. "Initial Observations" are issued October 20 – November 20, with templates for **Public** and **Roman Catholic** schools. School boards phased these in from 2016-17 — [Kindergarten Addendum](https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/policyfunding/growingSuccessAddendum.pdf); [ETFO bulletin](https://www.etfo.ca/about-us/member-advice/prs-matters-bulletins/growing-success-kindergarten-communication-of-learning)
- Competitors meet Ontario reporting needs: Veracross offers "OnSIS reporting for Ontario-based schools" — [Veracross Canada](https://www.veracross.com/canada/). Edsembli documents OnSIS for private schools — [Edsembli](https://sis.support.edsembli.com/en/support/solutions/articles/8000099278-march-onsis-for-inspected-private-schools)

### Inferences
- For JK–8 private schools, the decisive feature is a **report card that looks like theirs**. That might be a provincial-style layout (familiar to parents and to receiving high schools), a narrative/Montessori report, or a Quran/Islamic-studies section. It does not need to be "Ministry compliance." Wording such as "provincial-style report card" is safe; "Ministry-approved report card" is not, and it echoes the Ministry's own advertising restriction on schools.
- An "OnSIS-ready enrolment and staff export (October 31 counts)" would answer a real annual chore. Competitors advertise it, so its absence could be noticed.
- Schools that keep OSRs need printable hard-copy report cards and long retention. "Print/PDF every report card; export the full record" addresses that.
- The Kindergarten Communication of Learning is a public/Catholic-board instrument. Private JK/SK programs are not bound to it but may imitate it. That is consistent with SchoolMule's JK/SK competency grading.

### Gaps
- The Manual found is the 2013 edition (file dated 2021-11-25), and it notes revisions. I could not locate a newer consolidated edition or the revised OnSIS schedule.
- OEN eligibility comes from a vendor knowledge base, not Ministry text.
- I found no explicit Ministry statement on whether private schools use the Kindergarten CoL.

## 7. Data residency: do Canadian schools and parents expect Canadian hosting, and which provincial laws matter?

### Takeaway
Hard data-residency mandates in Canada apply to **public bodies**, mainly Nova Scotia's PIIDPA, and BC's mandate until 2021. They do **not** bind independent schools. BC independent schools fall under PIPA, which has no residency rule in what I read. Sentiment still favours Canadian hosting (vendor-sponsored 78% poll), and at least one Canadian competitor, Edsby, states Canadian hosting prominently. Veracross's Canada page says only "Hosted on AWS." Canadian hosting is a **differentiator and trust signal**, not a legal requirement for SchoolMule's buyers.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence
- BC's FOIPPA used to require public bodies and their service providers to store and access personal information only in Canada. Bill 22 (royal assent 2021-11-25) removed that requirement and replaced it with regulation-based rules for disclosure outside Canada. PIAs remain required — [Lawson Lundell](https://www.lawsonlundell.com/publication/the-new-changes-to-foippa-highlights-of-what-bc-public-bodies-need-to-know); [UBC Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.ubc.ca/bill22)
- BC independent schools are under PIPA, not FOIPPA — [BC Ministry of Education](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/program-management/independent-schools/other-legislation-regulations/personal-information-protection-act)
- Nova Scotia's PIIDPA (in force 2006-11-15 for government, **school boards**, universities and health authorities) requires public bodies and their service providers to store and access personal information only in Canada, with exceptions including consent and head-of-body approval. It does not apply to private-sector organizations "unless they are doing work on behalf of a public body." I did not verify the current consolidation — [NS PIIDPA FAQ](https://novascotia.ca/just/iap/piidpaquest.asp); [NS news release 2006](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2006/11/15/protection-privacy-legislation-proclaimed)
- Edsby: "Edsby customers in Canada have their Edsby cloud servers (including all computing resources, failover systems and data storage resources including backups) in Canada," on Microsoft Azure. "The education organization owns this data." ISO 27001 certified since August 2021 — [Edsby privacy & data](https://edsby.com/about-privacy-data/)
- Veracross Canada page: "Hosted on AWS," with no residency statement and no mention of PIPEDA — [Veracross Canada](https://www.veracross.com/canada/)

#### Hard-ish evidence (polls)
- 78% of Canadians refuse to have their data hosted outside Canada. Léger for OVHcloud, 2022, **vendor-sponsored** — [OVHcloud](https://corporate.ovhcloud.com/en/newsroom/news-survey-canadians-sovereignty-personal-data/)
- About two-thirds of Canadians were highly concerned about cross-border transfers via outsourcing to US companies (OPC/EKOS 2006, **old**) — [Government of Canada archive](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2006/06/poll-says-canadians-want-personal-information-treated-more-responsibly.html); see also [FIPA BC](https://fipa.bc.ca/majority-of-canadians-demand-informed-consent-on-cross-border-sharing-of-their-personal-information-4/)

### Inferences
- If SchoolMule's database and file storage are in a Canadian region, saying so specifically ("student data stored in Canada, in [region], including backups") is a strong trust signal. It is especially useful against US-hosted competitors such as Gradelink, FACTS and Veracross (AWS, unspecified region). If parts are not in Canada (for example email delivery or an AI provider), a sub-processor list is more credible than a blanket claim. I did not verify SchoolMule's actual hosting regions; this needs internal confirmation.
- Ontario private-school buyers are not legally bound to Canadian hosting. The pitch should be "your parents will ask; here's the answer," not "required by law."

### Gaps
- I found no survey of Canadian **private-school** leaders or parents on hosting location.
- I did not check whether PIIDPA has been amended since 2006, or whether other provinces (for example Newfoundland and Labrador) have relevant rules.

## 10. Islamic, faith-based and Montessori specifics

### Takeaway
Faith-based and Montessori schools need **non-standard assessment**:
- Islamic schools: Quran memorization (hifz), tajweed and Islamic-studies tracking;
- Montessori schools: narrative, observation-based records and photo updates.

They also have **community-specific norms**: photo consent with channel-level opt-outs and religious reservations about images, prayer-aware scheduling, and multilingual immigrant parents. They are **budget-constrained**, tuition-funded with no Ontario government funding, and founders cite sustainability challenges. Trust travels through **word of mouth**. Most evidence here is vendor marketing or single-school policy, so it is directional.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence (context)
- Ontario private schools receive no government funding — [Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf). - For Islamic schools, key website proof points would be:

  - correct display of Arabic names;
  - photo controls (per-student "no photo" flag, avatars or initials, parent-only visibility);

## 11. Website implications: how school leaders research software and what they look for on vendor sites

### Takeaway
Buyers increasingly research on their own before talking to sales:
- 61% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience (Gartner 2025, cross-industry);
- 48% of educators look at vendor sites despite distrusting vendor research (ISTE 2018);
- K–12 leaders call opaque quotes "a waste of time" (EdWeek 2025, **US, mostly district**).

They want, before booking a demo:
- transparent pricing;
- clear promises and timelines (onboarding plan);
- references from similar schools;
- security and residency facts;
- proof of support quality.

Video (YouTube) is a notable discovery channel for US district buyers.

### Cited Findings

#### Hard evidence (surveys)
- 61% of B2B buyers prefer an overall rep-free buying experience. Buyers prefer self-service for general information and seek seller input to judge fit. 73% avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach. Gartner survey of 632 buyers, fielded Aug–Sep 2024, released 2025-06-25. **Cross-industry, not education** — [Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)
- ISTE/Jefferson Education Exchange survey of 1,124 educators (2018): 76% felt ed-tech vendors were not qualified to conduct valid research about their products, yet 48% still look at vendors' sites for information. Educators rarely consult the What Works Clearinghouse. **US** — [Education Week, 2018-08-29](https://edweek.org/ew/articles/2018/08/29/educators-wary-of-ed-tech-company-research.html)
- EdWeek Market Brief 2022-23 purchasing report (survey of 2,600 **district** staff): YouTube "has emerged as a key social media resource for district decision-makers researching products," ahead of Twitter, Facebook and LinkedIn — [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/special-report/school-district-purchasing-priorities/)
- EdWeek Market Brief has a survey on what districts expect from companies on "clarity and transparency of their products' costs and supports." It is paywalled and I could not read the figures — [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/exclusive-data/what-k-12-districts-expect-from-companies-pricing/)
- K–12 leaders' advice to vendors (Feb 2025, "a couple hundred"): "Contacting a vendor for a mysterious quote is a waste of time"; "Be clear in promises, pricing, and timelines"; "I will buy from vendors I trust" — [EdWeek WirED Marketer](https://www.edweek.org/advertise/the-wired-marketer-advice-from-k-12-leaders-to-vendors-they-dont-mince-words-2-25-25/2025/03)
- For international schools, "free trials, testimonials matter" (EdWeek Market Brief headline; article not read) — [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/marketplace-k-12/trying-bring-product-international-schools-free-trials-testimonials-matter/)

### Inferences
Pre-demo information checklist for the SchoolMule site (synthesis):
1. **Pricing**: a price, or a calculator for 30–200 students, including setup/migration and "no add-on fees" (texting, modules, payments).
2. **Security & privacy page**, mapped to the IPC/OIPC PowerSchool recommendations: data location (Canada, if true), MFA, access logging, limited support access, breach-response commitment, DPA/contract terms, data ownership and export, PIPEDA-principles framing.
3. **"Switching to SchoolMule"**: migration from spreadsheets or an old SIS, who does it, a timeline (aligned to Faria's 3–5 months and a summer cutover), and a "ready for September" promise.
4. **Report cards**: real samples (provincial-style, JK/SK competency, narrative, Islamic studies), with the honest note that Ontario private schools may choose their format.
5. **Parents**: email-first, no app required; a screenshot of a parent email; translation or language support if available.
6. **Teachers**: training approach and time-to-first-gradebook; short video walkthroughs (YouTube matters to US buyers).
7. **References from similar schools**: named Ontario Islamic or independent schools, an offer to "talk to a principal who uses it," and size-matched case studies.
8. **Support**: named humans, response times, Canadian time zone.
9. **Continuity**: data export and what happens if SchoolMule ends, which answers the Engrade-style fear.
10. **Self-serve path**: a sandbox or demo video, since Gartner's buyers prefer self-service before talking to sales.
