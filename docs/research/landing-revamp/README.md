# Make schoolmule.ca earn a principal's trust

> **In this folder**
> - **README.md** (this report): what to change on schoolmule.ca and why, with every recommendation traced to evidence.
> - [teardown-table.md](teardown-table.md): one table of all 53 site teardowns, with a link to each desktop fold screenshot. The raw data is in [teardowns.csv](teardowns.csv) (the four teardown CSVs merged under one header).
> - [positioning-map.svg](positioning-map.svg) and [positioning-map.md](positioning-map.md): where 25 competitors and SchoolMule sit, the evidence for each placement, and the open space.
> - [borrow-list.md](borrow-list.md): 24 patterns to borrow (source, evidence, where it goes on our site, confidence), plus the anti-patterns we will not copy.
> - [notes/](notes/): the research notes this report is built from. [screenshots/](screenshots/): every capture, including the current site in [schoolmule-before/](screenshots/schoolmule-before/).

SchoolMule should rebuild schoolmule.ca around **honest, local, peer-scale proof** and a **tiny, instant booking path**, not around a bigger product tour. The current homepage opens with "Trusted by 50+ Schools Nationwide" and a "Start Free Trial" button, but the code shows five schools in the sign-up directory and no trial at all, while outreach emails tell the same principals SchoolMule is "in use at several Ontario Islamic schools" ([feature verification](notes/feature-verification.md), [dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). That mismatch lands at the exact moment a principal decides whether to trust a small vendor, and 69% of B2B buyers already report seeing inconsistencies between vendor websites and what sellers say ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)). The buyer is a principal-operator at a small Islamic or Montessori school, usually in the GTA, usually with early years, and usually with **no real system at all**: 49 prospects were observed running on Google Forms, PDFs, Gmail and WhatsApp against only 19 with a named SIS ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). Across 53 marketing sites torn down, the best performers show the real product in the first screen, keep long films one click away, route each role to its own path, publish prices when they sell to small operators, and book demos with almost no form; the school-software category mostly does the opposite, and **no all-in-one vendor combines "built for small Ontario independent schools", a visible price and real product screens** ([teardown table](teardown-table.md), [positioning map](positioning-map.md)). The conversion evidence is thinner than marketing blogs suggest (almost nothing is A-grade and B2B), so the redesign should ship with measurement, starting with the fact that the marketing site has no analytics today.

## Executive summary: twelve findings that shape the redesign

1. **Fix the claims before the design.** Today's site states "50+ Schools", "10,000+ Students", "99.9% Uptime", FERPA and COPPA compliance, multi-factor auth, automated invoicing and attendance alerts, shows two testimonials from schools that are not customers, and sends "Start Free Trial" to a directory of existing schools. All of these are false or unverified in code ([feature verification §3](notes/feature-verification.md); fold: [desktop](screenshots/schoolmule-before/home-desktop-fold.jpg), [phone](screenshots/schoolmule-before/home-mobile-fold.jpg)). One approved proof statement should be shared by the site and the email templates.
2. **Sell "your first real system", not "switch your SIS".** Of 315 prospect schools analysed, 115 are Islamic and 104 Montessori, the median known enrolment is about 130, 83% run early years, 56% are in the GTA, and 69% show no system publicly; where tools were observed they are free form-and-inbox tools ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)).
3. **The open space is specific.** Among the 13 SIS-category vendors only Sycamore and SchoolCues publish prices and none shows an Ontario-style report card, a parent's grade view on a phone or a kindergarten skills report; MySchool claims "Built For Canadian Schools" for mid-to-large schools, and Ad-Din owns Ontario Islamic schools with Ministry-style report cards ([SIS teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-sis.md), [comms teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-comms.md)). The unclaimed position is small (30–200) Ontario JK–8 schools of every type, with price on the page and real screens ([positioning map](positioning-map.md)).
4. **Privacy is the Ontario buying issue, and SchoolMule must win it on candour.** The PowerSchool breach affected about 3.86 million Ontarians and the privacy commissioners published what boards should have demanded of vendors ([Hicks Morley](https://hicksmorley.com/2025/11/20/final-report-released-on-powerschool-cyberattack/), [OIPC Alberta](https://oipc.ab.ca/ontario-and-alberta-privacy-commissioners-release-investigation-findings-into-powerschool-breach-affecting-school-boards-and-other-educational-bodies/)). SchoolMule's database is in AWS Ohio and its API in Virginia, so it **cannot claim Canadian data residency**; it also has no MFA yet ([feature verification §2](notes/feature-verification.md)). A plain-language security page that states US hosting and closes the MFA gap first beats any badge.
5. **Put a price on the page.** Buyers rank transparent pricing as their top wish ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/blog/tech-buyers-demand-honest-and-up-front-pricing-info)); in one observational dataset, sites with visible pricing turned 17.5% of form fills into pipeline against 10.3% for hidden pricing ([HockeyStack](https://hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/state-of-pricing-demo-case-study-pages)); cost is the top deal-breaker for 63% of education buyers ([Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/business/the-state-of-school-purchasing-report-2026)); and 21 of 24 SchoolMule call scripts anticipate "too small / can't afford it" ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)).
6. **Make booking instant and tiny.** Vendor datasets show roughly 62–65% of form-fillers book when they can pick a slot on submit, against an unsourced 30–40% for manual follow-up ([RevenueHero](https://revenuehero.io/2025-inbound-benchmark), [Chili Piper](https://help.chilipiper.com/hc/en-us/articles/33750048681875--Jan-2024-The-Sauce-What-s-a-good-demo-conversion-rate)); an optional phone field cut sign-ups 37% in a controlled test ([NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/?p=14159)); and a one-line privacy reassurance raised sign-ups by half in another ([NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/?p=12850)). SchoolMule already embeds Calendly; it needs one stated length and no extra fields.
7. **Keep the film, but stop making phones download it.** Across about 35,000 landing pages, video did not consistently lift conversion and tended to hurt click-through pages on both mobile and desktop ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-optimization/video-on-landing-pages)); faster pages convert better ([Portent](https://www.portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyone.htm), [Vodafone](https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone)). None of the 13 YC sites autoplays a narrated film in the hero; the best put it behind a labelled play button with its length (Loom, Toast) or in a chaptered modal (Campfire). The current hero loads a 10 MB 720p loop on phones.
8. **Use the four portals as the navigation, not just the story.** Role tiles (ClassDojo, Brightwheel), a chaptered film (Campfire, Superhuman), and a single "one person, one record" card (Rippling, Veracross) make "one database, every view" visible and route parents who came looking for the login away from the sales path ([YC teardowns](notes/teardowns-yc.md), [comms teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-comms.md)). This rests on convergent practice, not measured lift.
9. **Proof has to be named, local and the right size.** Education buyers trust peers most (72%) ([Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/business/the-state-of-school-purchasing-report-2026)); real faces raise perceived trust ([Cyr et al.](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol33/iss3/8)) and stock people are ignored ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)). With one or a few live schools, one named principal with photo, school, size and city plus a visible founder beats any logo wall.
10. **Design the phone page as its own page.** Education landing traffic is about 85% mobile, yet desktop converts 17.6% better ([Unbounce Education](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/education-conversion-rate/)). Competitors' phone folds are often broken by pop-ups or have no button ([Veracross](screenshots/category/veracross-mobile-fold.jpg), [SchoolCues](screenshots/category/schoolcues-mobile-fold.jpg)); the clean ones show headline, button and product ([Toddle](screenshots/category/toddleapp-mobile-fold.jpg)).
11. **Lead with what is real and distinctive.** Publish & Notify grade emails with "Ask the teacher", reply reminders, skill-based JK/SK reports, Ontario-style report cards, the optional student portal with quizzes, the timetable generator and staff hours with Ontario stat holidays are all shipped. Absence alerts, invoicing, French, Arabic/RTL, a native app and Montessori narrative reporting are not ([feature verification §1](notes/feature-verification.md)).
12. **Measure before optimising.** The marketing site has no analytics at all ([feature verification §3.3](notes/feature-verification.md)), and no outreach reply, demo or loss has been logged ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). Email-sourced visits convert far higher than other channels (14.1% median in education) ([Unbounce Education](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/education-conversion-rate/)), so the page that cold emails link to is the highest-leverage surface.

## Fifty-three sites agree on product, phone and price; most school vendors miss all three

Every site was captured on 2026-10-25 with [tools/shoot.mjs](tools/shoot.mjs) at desktop 1440×900 and phone 390×844, plus a JSON record of headings, CTAs and video use. The full row-by-row comparison is in [teardown-table.md](teardown-table.md).

### YC companies keep the film one click away and cut demo friction to almost nothing

The 13 YC companies were verified against their ycombinator.com pages (for example [ClassDojo, Summer 2012](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/classdojo) and [Scout, Winter 2025](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/scout-4), an "AI-powered student information system for K-12 schools" and SchoolMule's own category). **None autoplays a narrated product film in the hero.** Hero motion is either an ambient loop (Resend's 10-second cube, Pylon's 25-second abstract background, Numeral's 30-second globe) or animated product UI built into the page (Vanta, PostHog, Flint's live sample school "Oak Hill Academy" with a four-step guided tour, [fold](screenshots/yc/flint-desktop-fold.jpg)). Full films sit behind a secondary button: Campfire's ungated chaptered modal ("Select a chapter below to view by product, or press play to start from the beginning", [screenshot](screenshots/yc/campfire-tour-modal.jpg)), PostHog's 8:49 demo with muted autoplay and "Click for sound", Flint's 54-second Vimeo mid-page, and Rippling's 10:47 tour hidden behind a six-field form ([campfire.ai](https://campfire.ai), [posthog.com/demo](https://posthog.com/demo), [rippling tour](https://www.rippling.com/en-CA/tour-video)).

Demo friction ranges from **zero fields to nine**: Scout links "Schedule a demo" straight to a 30-minute Calendly with no form on the site, Avoca asks for one business email, Flint two fields, Pylon five (showing a blurred calendar captioned "Fill in the form and unlock your booking time" and stating "a personalized 30-minute demo"), Rippling seven and Vanta nine plus checkboxes ([scoutforschools.com](https://scoutforschools.com), [avoca demo](https://www.avoca.ai/demo), [pylon schedule-demo](https://www.usepylon.com/schedule-demo), [vanta demo](https://www.vanta.com/demo)). Pricing splits cleanly by buyer: owner-operator and education sellers publish it (Gusto, Numeral, Pylon, and Flint's school tiers of $3,000, $4,000 and $6,500 a year for up to 150, 250 and 500 users, with "Funding shouldn't be a barrier. Let's talk."), while enterprise demo-led sellers hide it ([flint pricing](https://flintk12.com/pricing), [screenshot](screenshots/yc/flint-pricing-desktop-fold.jpg)).

The three education companies are the most directly useful. **Scout** puts a region pill above its headline ("for California and Oregon schools and districts") and shows each job as a sample-data UI card with an "Old way" comparison ("Onboard a student in 4 minutes, not 4 weeks") ([fold](screenshots/yc/scout-desktop-fold.jpg), [scroll sheet](screenshots/yc/scout-scroll-sheet.jpg)). **Flint** runs dedicated faith pages ("Flint for Christian schools", plus Jewish schools) with segment logos and the same film ([christian-schools](https://flintk12.com/christian-schools), [screenshot](screenshots/yc/flint-christian-schools-desktop-fold.jpg)). **ClassDojo** puts five role tiles under its headline ("Get started as a..." Teacher / Parent / Student / School Leader / District Leader) and swaps them for a single "Sign up" button on phones ([desktop](screenshots/yc/classdojo-desktop-fold.jpg), [phone](screenshots/yc/classdojo-mobile-fold.jpg)). Among the non-education companies, Rippling proves "one system" with a single employee record card under "Your business data with people at the center", Numeral has a section called "The human layer" (guarantee, concierge onboarding, experts), and Vanta's security page is a clean skeleton of principles, data protection, testing, people and vendor security, and privacy ([rippling](https://www.rippling.com), [numeral](https://www.numeral.com), [vanta security](https://www.vanta.com/company/security), [screenshot](screenshots/yc/vanta-security-desktop-fold.jpg)).

### Best-in-class SaaS shows the real product first and designs the phone separately

The 15 SaaS homepages converge on one pattern: **the real product in the first screen**, either rebuilt as live HTML (Linear, Attio, Cal.com, whose hero is a working booking widget for "Academic Counseling") or as a short muted loop (Ramp, Notion, Toast), with any longer film behind a play button ([SaaS teardowns](notes/teardowns-saas.md)). Loom frames its hero video in a real player with a time-cost badge ("1.2× · 1 min → 50 sec") ([fold](screenshots/saas/loom-desktop-fold.jpg)); Toast pairs a muted kitchen loop with a "▶ How Toast is Built For Busy" pill for the brand film ([fold](screenshots/saas/toast-desktop-fold.jpg)).

The three sites that sell to owner-operators, the closest analogue to a principal running a busy school, add what the developer tools skip. Jobber's hero is a strip of real tradespeople captioned with their names and businesses, a "4.5 ★ 4,883 Google Play Reviews" line under the buttons, and a toll-free number in the nav ([fold](screenshots/saas/jobber-desktop-fold.jpg)). Gusto swaps its desktop dashboard for a phone-app screen on phones and closes with four doors by readiness, one of which reads "See plans and pricing: As in real numbers on the page. No proposal or quote needed" ([gusto](https://gusto.com), [phone](screenshots/saas/gusto-mobile-fold.jpg)). Toast's menu carries two featured cards for the two big entry situations, "Switch to Toast" and "New openings" ([megamenu](screenshots/saas/toast-desktop-megamenu.jpg)).

Two demo patterns stand out. Ramp's demo page offers **three doors**: a 30-minute live demo booked with one email field, a two-minute interactive tour, and five short walkthrough videos; its Canadian site also says "Built for Canadian finance teams" and adds a Canadian FAQ ([ramp see-a-demo](https://ramp.com/see-a-demo), [screenshot](screenshots/saas/ramp-demo-desktop-fold.jpg)). Mercury's ungated demo is the real product with fake data and a role switcher, "Viewing as Admin → View as Employee" ([demo.mercury.com](https://demo.mercury.com/dashboard), [screenshot](screenshots/saas/mercury-demo-desktop-fold.jpg)). On phones, the best sites change the offer rather than shrinking it: Attio replaces "Start for free" with an email field and **"Send me a demo"**, Stripe and Toast drop their subheads, Loom drops its desktop-only extension button, and Toast adds a fixed "Get a Free Demo" bar (found in the page code) and a sales number in its phone header ([attio](https://attio.com), [stripe](https://stripe.com/en-ca), [toast](https://pos.toasttab.com)).

### The school-software category gates the product, hides the price and barely names Ontario

The 25 category sites (13 SIS and school-management vendors, 12 communication, classroom, Montessori and faith tools) behave almost uniformly. **Almost every school-management vendor gates the product behind a sales demo**, with forms of 7 to 11 fields and no inline scheduler on any page checked (Veracross 10, Gradelink 10, SchoolCues 11, MySchool 10, Alma 9, Blackbaud 7, Edsby 7 plus consent) ([SIS teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-sis.md)). Gradelink even calls a gated recorded video a "demo" ([screenshot](screenshots/category/gradelink-demo-desktop-fold.jpg)). An embedded calendar with two or three fields would be shorter than every competitor seen.

**Prices are public only at the small end.** Among the 13 SIS-category vendors, only Sycamore ("$4 per student per month with every module included", with worked examples from 50 to 1,000 students, an 18-month minimum and a $2,500 onboarding fee) and SchoolCues ("As low as $75.00/mo for a 12-month school year") publish numbers ([sycamore pricing](https://sycamoreleaf.com/pricing/), [screenshot](screenshots/category/sycamore-pricing-desktop-fold.jpg), [schoolcues](https://www.schoolcues.com/)). The small Montessori and faith tools all publish per-child prices: Transparent Classroom $2.00 USD, Montessori Compass $2.00 USD or **$2.50 CAD** on its Canada page, Educa $1.29–$1.59, Alif Cloud free to 30 students then $1.49 ([transparent classroom](https://www.transparentclassroom.com/), [montessori compass canada](https://www.montessoricompass.com/canada/), [educa pricing](https://www.geteduca.com/pricing/), [alif pricing](https://www.alifcloud.com/pricing)). District and childcare-chain vendors hide them behind "Contact sales", "Get a quote" or a six-step quiz (Brightwheel).

**No SIS vendor shows the artefacts a JK–8 principal actually produces**: no Ontario-style report card, no parent grade view on a phone, no kindergarten skills report ([SIS teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-sis.md)); among the communication and faith tools, only SchoolCues shows real parent-app phone screens ([comms teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-comms.md)). Among the SIS vendors, Toddle alone has a real-UI hero, and it has the cleanest phone fold in the set ([phone](screenshots/category/toddleapp-mobile-fold.jpg)). The incumbents lead with abstract or scale claims a 90-student school cannot test: "Technology with Heart. Powered by Data." (FACTS), "More Power Stronger School" for "over 60 million students in 90 countries" (PowerSchool), "Essential K–12 Private School Software—Powered by AI" (Blackbaud) ([factsmgt](https://factsmgt.com/), [powerschool](https://www.powerschool.com/), [blackbaud k-12](https://www.blackbaud.com/who-we-serve/k-12-schools)).

**Canada is claimed thinly, with three exceptions.** Veracross shows Canadian visitors a pop-up ("Built for Canadian Private and Independent Schools… over a dozen independent schools") that, on a phone, covers about 70% of the screen and hides the headline ([screenshot](screenshots/category/veracross-mobile-fold.jpg)). FACTS preselects Canada in a country picker, SchoolCues swaps the noun on a templated "Canadian Schools" page, and PowerSchool's Canada page speaks only to boards. The exceptions are the competitors to watch. **MySchool** puts "Built For Canadian Schools" in its H1 and backs it with a four-card block naming OnSIS, OUAC and OCAS reporting, Canadian hosting, PIPEDA and CAD invoicing; it is a CIS Ontario sponsor, mostly serves mid-to-large schools, and lists a Malta address ([trymyschool](https://www.trymyschool.com/), [fold](screenshots/category/trymyschool-desktop-fold.jpg)). **Montessori Compass** has a Canada page saying "Your student data stays in Canada", billing in CAD and a Montessori Canada offer, plus a top bar promising "We'll help migrate your data—free" ([fold](screenshots/category/montessori-compass-canada-desktop-fold.jpg)). **Ad-Din**, the confirmed incumbent at one SchoolMule prospect, sells "Ontario-Aligned Academic Management… designed to meet Ontario (Canada) Ministry of Education Reporting Mandates" to mosques, madrasahs and Islamic schools, with a logo wall labelled by Ontario city ([ad-din.ca](https://www.ad-din.ca/), [fold](screenshots/category/ad-din-desktop-fold.jpg)). Edsby has the deepest Ontario proof of all (quotes from York Region, Durham Catholic, St. Michael's College School and others) but never says "Ontario" on its homepage ([edsby](https://www.edsby.com/)).

Review sites tell the same story from the buyer's side, though every rating below came from search snippets because G2 and Capterra blocked direct fetching, so none should be quoted publicly without rechecking. The big suites are "capable but complex" for small schools: a small school called PowerSchool "Good but too much for us… too difficult for a small school", and Software Advice lists Blackbaud K–12 from $21,000 a year ([Capterra PowerSchool](https://capterra.com/p/154883/PowerSchool-Student-Information-System/reviews/), [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/k-12/blackbaud-k-12-profile/)). The cheap tools are praised for affordability and support and criticised as dated: Capterra's summary for Gradelink pairs "Responsive and supportive staff team" with "Outdated and unintuitive interface design" ([Capterra Gradelink](https://capterra.com/p/118900/Gradelink-SIS/)). SchoolMule can make both contrasts without naming anyone: not a district system cut down, not a newsletter app built up.

## Small schools buy on trust, price and fit, and the decision runs through the principal

### The principal-operator decides, a board often signs, and September sets the calendar

In Ontario, private schools "operate as businesses or non-profit organizations, independently of the Ministry", which "does not regulate, licence, accredit or otherwise oversee the day-to-day operation of private schools", and their principals need not hold Ontario principal's qualifications ([Private Schools Policy and Procedures Manual](https://files.ontario.ca/edu-private-schools-policy-procedures-manual-2013-en-2021-11-25.pdf)). The buyer is therefore the operator: a founder or owner, a (often volunteer) board, and the principal, with an office manager running the system day to day. SchoolMule's own dossiers confirm the shape. The principal is the named contact at about 70% of schools (partly because the provincial registry lists one for every school), 26 schools list one person as both founder or owner and principal, named office or registrar staff are rare, and **46% of Islamic dossiers describe a school run inside a masjid, Islamic centre or association board** ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). A principal at those schools may have to sell SchoolMule to a board, which argues for a forwardable one-page overview with price, security and a reference.

The market is the middle of the sector, not a niche: Cardus counted at least 1,445 Ontario independent schools serving over 154,000 students, an average of about 107 students per school (our arithmetic), and reports that 322 new independent schools opened and stayed open between 2022 and 2025, "the majority of which are small", many of them Islamic ([Cardus 2022](https://old.cardus.ca/news/news-releases/number-of-independent-schools-in-ontario-up-52-since-2013), [CP24](https://cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/07/18/if-the-gta-population-is-up-why-are-public-school-numbers-down)). Every private school must file a Notice of Intention to Operate by **September 1** ([ontario.ca](https://ontario.ca/page/private-schools-notice-intention-operate-private-school)), and the cleanest moment to switch an SIS is the summer between years after a three-to-five-month implementation ([Faria](https://www.faria.org/insights/switching-software-mid-year-vs-between-cycles-how-to-time-it-right/), a vendor source). The inferred buying calendar is evaluate January–April, sign April–June, migrate July–August, go live in September, with a separate "starting a school, get set up before September" path for the new schools. Education buyers also evaluate year-round (67%), so booking should always be open ([Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/business/the-state-of-school-purchasing-report-2026)).

### Data security tops the fears, followed by cost, adoption and "what if you disappear"

The best-evidenced Canadian fear is student-data security. In the PowerSchool breach (22–28 December 2024), about 3.86 million Ontarians were affected, and the Ontario and Alberta commissioners found that boards had left privacy and security terms out of contracts, failed to monitor vendor safeguards including remote-support access and MFA, and lacked breach-response plans ([Hicks Morley](https://hicksmorley.com/2025/11/20/final-report-released-on-powerschool-cyberattack/), [OIPC Alberta](https://oipc.ab.ca/ontario-and-alberta-privacy-commissioners-release-investigation-findings-into-powerschool-breach-affecting-school-boards-and-other-educational-bodies/)). A federal finding in 2021 put Edsby's vendor on record for not having "a robust, over-arching information security framework", citing weak parent passwords and unprotected student photo thumbnails ([OPC 2021-002](https://priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2021/pipeda-2021-002)). These cases involved public boards, but private-school parents read the same news, and the commissioners' recommendations are the clearest public checklist a principal can hold a vendor to. A small vendor that publishes how it meets each item can turn "small vendor means risky" into "small vendor means accountable". School leaders say they will scrutinise vendors' cybersecurity more (74%) ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/k-12-leaders-to-scrutinize-ed-tech-vendor-security-practices)).

The other fears are documented mostly through reviews and anecdotes. Cost and add-on fees recur (Brightwheel reviewers complain that cost "grows with the number of parents"; a Gradelink reviewer wants texting "without an additional fee") ([Capterra brightwheel](https://capterra.com/p/144060/brightwheel/reviews/?page=3), [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/gradelink/)). Parent uptake favours email over apps in US data (83% of parents prefer personal email for teacher communication, 39% a mobile app) ([EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/2019-10-15-project-tomorrow-survey-in-an-app-world-email-still-reaches-parents)). Vendor continuity is a real risk even at big owners: McGraw-Hill shut down the Engrade gradebook it had bought ([DSST](https://blog.dsstpublicschools.org/helix/important-engrade-update)). Across review snippets for eight products, **support is the most consistent praise and report cards the most consistent complaint** (inflexible formats, calling support to change a layout) ([buyer research §4](notes/buyer-research.md)). SchoolMule's size becomes a strength if the site says "you talk to the person who builds it" and means it.

### The questions to answer before the call

Vendor-published question lists converge on total cost, support responsiveness, ease of use per role, a demo or trial, privacy compliance, references and hosting ([FACTS](https://factsmgt.com/blog/key-questions-to-ask-when-selecting-a-student-information-system/), [Edsembli](https://edsembli.com/8-key-questions-to-ask-when-choosing-a-new-sis-solution/)). K–12 leaders are blunter: "Contacting a vendor for a mysterious quote is a waste of time" and "Be clear in promises, pricing, and timelines" ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/advertise/the-wired-marketer-advice-from-k-12-leaders-to-vendors-they-dont-mince-words-2-25-25/2025/03)). Gartner's buyers prefer a rep-free experience overall (61%) but still want a seller's input on fit, and the eventual winner was on the day-one shortlist 95% of the time in 6sense's data, so the site must answer these questions on its own and make the call a confirmation ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience), [6sense](https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/)). The ranked list below is a synthesis of the buyer research, not a survey result.

| # | Likely question from a small Ontario principal | Where the site should answer it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What does it cost all-in for my number of students? Any setup, migration or texting fees? | Price block on the homepage and a pricing page with worked examples |
| 2 | Where is our data stored, who can see it, and what happens if you are breached? | Plain-language security page (US hosting stated) |
| 3 | Can you import our spreadsheets or old system, and who does it? | "How switching works" in three steps |
| 4 | Will my teachers manage it? How long is training? | Teacher section with time-to-first-gradebook (founder to confirm) |
| 5 | Will parents use it? Email only? Phone? Other languages? | Parent section: email-first, one-tap sign-in, no app needed |
| 6 | Can it produce our report card? | Real sample report cards (JK/SK skills, grades 1–8) |
| 7 | Who else like us uses it, and can I call them? | Named reference school and "talk to a principal who uses it" |
| 8 | What if you shut down? Can we export everything? | Continuity and export promise on the security page |

### Islamic schools carry most of the observed pain; Montessori needs recognition, not asserted pain

Islamic schools sell "Ontario curriculum plus Islamic studies, Quran and Arabic", values and longevity; their research in the dossiers is roughly twice as deep and contains almost all of the observed pain: registration on Google Forms or Jotform, waitlists (26 schools), Hifz tracks next to Ontario report cards (26), parent communication through WhatsApp, Telegram or Canva updates, and "Coming Soon" parent portals ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). Al-Qasim Academy runs a K–8 day school "entirely off Google Forms" with WhatsApp for parents; the Islamic School of Hamilton reports "over 250 students enrolled and roughly 300 students on the waitlist" with no SIS. Faith-specific norms also matter: photo consent with opt-outs and religious reservations about images, and multilingual immigrant parents ([buyer research §10](notes/buyer-research.md)). SchoolMule already hides student photos until a school turns them on, which is a real feature to surface ([feature verification](notes/feature-verification.md)). Ad-Din is the Islamic-specific competitor, with a free tier, Hifz fields and Ministry-style report cards, so the Islamic page must show SchoolMule's Ontario-style report cards and a named Ontario Islamic reference, not just say "built for Islamic schools". The dossiers also warn that the Ontario Islamic segment is close to saturated (113 contacted; GTA marked exhausted), so new growth must come from Montessori and independents.

Montessori schools sell authentic method and accreditation, the Casa-to-elementary continuum and heritage; they say "Casa", not "JK/SK" (91 of 104 run Casa or toddler programs) ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). Their Montessori-native incumbents (Transparent Classroom, OwlWise) and the category leaders on the web (Montessori Compass, Transparent Classroom) win on observation records, lesson grids and photos, at about $2 per child per month. Montessori pain in the dossiers is mostly a templated hypothesis, and the "narrative, skill-based assessment" promised in about 109 Montessori email drafts was **not found in code** ([dossier synthesis §7](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). The Montessori page should therefore lead with recognition of the model and the operations layer those tools lack (attendance, report cards, parent messaging, staff), and should not promise narrative reporting until it exists.

## The conversion evidence is mostly B-grade vendor data, so test what matters

Almost no A-grade, B2B-specific, demo-booking evidence is public; the strongest material is observational vendor data and buyer surveys, and most A-grade results transfer from nonprofits, e-commerce or lab psychology ([conversion research](notes/conversion-research.md)). Grades follow the notes: **A** randomised or controlled test with sample reported; **B** large-sample observational benchmark or survey; **C** vendor case study or single-company anecdote; **D** expert opinion. (T) marks a transfer from another setting; (V) marks data from a vendor that sells the intervention.

| Question | Best evidence | Grade | What it means for SchoolMule |

| Do extra fields hurt? | Optional phone field: 99.6% → 62.9% (−36.9%), n = 1,454 ([NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/?p=14159)); phone, age and address fields associated with lower conversion ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx)); field count itself weakly linked to booking ([RevenueHero](https://revenuehero.io/2025-inbound-benchmark)) | A (T) / B | No phone field; at most one optional context field (school size) |

Keep the homepage short: Unbounce's education median target is 363 words, so sections 4–11 should each be a heading, one or two sentences and a visual, with depth on linked pages ([Unbounce Education](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/education-conversion-rate/)). Write at about grade 6–8 and mirror the schools' own language ("every child known", "from Casa through Grade 8", "lift the load"), avoiding words like "nightmare" or "drowning" that clash with the respect-first voice already used in outreach ([dossier synthesis §5](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)).

### The demo page offers three doors and books in under a minute

Model it on Ramp's three doors: **book a call** (embedded Calendly, name and email only, optional "number of students", one stated length, slots within one to three days, a privacy line such as "Your details go only to the founder. No mailing list."), **watch the 1:41 tour** with chapters and captions, and **see a sample report card** (PDF). A Mercury-style "View as Principal / Teacher / Parent" demo school is the stretch goal; the Playground seed school exists, but exposing it would need a read-only, auto-reset sandbox, and 88% of interactive-demo sessions are on desktop anyway ([Navattic](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025)). Add an Attio-style "Send me the tour" email option on phones for principals who will watch later at a desk. The Calendly booking runs on the founder's personal handle and is not mentioned in the privacy policy ([feature verification §3.2](notes/feature-verification.md)); fix both.

### The film becomes a chaptered, click-to-play asset

The film's chapters already map onto the site: 0:08–0:15 "One school. Every view." is the hero loop candidate; 0:15–0:38 (teacher), 0:38–0:54 (student), 0:54–1:09 and 1:09–1:17 (parent and the teacher's reply) and 1:21–1:28 (admin montage) become the four tile targets. On phones, show a poster frame and a "▶ 1:41" button instead of loading the 10 MB 720p file; on desktop, a muted loop of the "Every view" segment is acceptable if it does not delay the largest paint. Add captions (PostHog shows captions and a speed control), label the footage "Demo school, sample data" (PostHog labels its demo data), put "Book a demo" inside the modal (Campfire), and track plays and completions. Two content cautions from the code audit: the AI weekly summary shown at 0:54 is real but not opt-in and sends data to OpenAI, so disclose it on the privacy page; and the phone chapter must not suggest an installable app.

### Pricing, security and segment pages

**Pricing page:** a per-student price in CAD with worked examples for 50, 100, 150 and 200 students (Sycamore's format), what is included, contract length, setup or migration fees if any, and "closed in summer?" fairness rules if the founder wants them (Transparent Classroom charges nothing in months a school is closed). Avoid Montessori Compass's mistake: its homepage example says "50 active students = $50/month", contradicting its own formula ([comms teardowns](notes/teardowns-category-comms.md)).

**Security and privacy page:** follow Vanta's skeleton in Notion's plain cards, mapped to the commissioners' checklist: where data lives (US, named providers), who can access it (roles, staff-only support access, read-only "View as"), how it is protected (hashing, signed links, encrypted integration tokens), what happens in a breach (a written response commitment), what AI sees, and what happens when you leave (export, return, deletion). Publish it only after MFA, a stronger password rule, a backup plan and a corrected privacy page exist; otherwise it invites the questions it cannot answer ([vanta security](screenshots/yc/vanta-security-desktop-fold.jpg), [notion security](screenshots/saas/notion-security-desktop-fold.jpg)).

**Segment pages, in this order:** Islamic schools (Ontario curriculum plus Quran, Hifz, Arabic and Islamic Studies as report-card subjects, JK/SK, waitlists and registration forms, photo controls, a board-ready one-pager, a named Ontario Islamic reference); independent schools (individual attention, IEP-heavy and bilingual programs); Montessori (Casa language, the operations layer, no narrative-reporting promise until built). Each must carry real specifics; SchoolCues' noun-swapped pages show what not to do ([screenshot](screenshots/category/schoolcues-islamic-desktop-fold.jpg)). Persona-page lift data is vendor-only, so the case rests on message match with outreach emails.

### Measure from day one

Add privacy-respecting analytics to the marketing pages (none exist today), track Calendly's booked-event signal as the primary goal, tag outreach links by segment, log replies and demos in the pipeline, and judge pricing visibility by demo-to-customer rate rather than booking volume ([conversion research](notes/conversion-research.md), [feature verification §3.3](notes/feature-verification.md)). Fix the technical basics at the same time: per-page titles and canonicals (every page currently canonicalises to the homepage), an Open Graph image, `/privacy` and `/terms` in the sitemap, and every new marketing path added to `WEBSITE_PATHS` so signed-in users are not redirected away.

## Method and limits

Teardowns: 53 sites (13 YC, 15 SaaS, 25 category) captured with [tools/shoot.mjs](tools/shoot.mjs) on 2026-10-25 from a Canadian locale; motion was judged from stills, DOM video attributes and scroll captures; Edsby's visuals were blocked by a bot check and Maplewood's site was down; no forms were submitted ([YC](notes/teardowns-yc.md), [SaaS](notes/teardowns-saas.md), [SIS](notes/teardowns-category-sis.md), [comms](notes/teardowns-category-comms.md)). Buyer research relied on Ontario regulation, regulator findings and surveys, with review quotes from search snippets because review sites blocked fetching ([buyer research](notes/buyer-research.md)). Conversion evidence was graded A–D ([conversion research](notes/conversion-research.md)). SchoolMule's 316 prospect dossiers (315 schools analysed) were mined by script and read in full for pain points; most enrolments are estimates and no buyer responses are logged ([dossier synthesis](notes/dossier-synthesis.md)). Product facts come from code on both main branches and Railway configuration, without querying the production database ([feature verification](notes/feature-verification.md)). No source in this research measures demo-booking rates for school software specifically, so every "borrow" is a fit judgement until SchoolMule measures its own.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "redesign" means for schoolmule.ca. The craft gap to the best SaaS sites is real but secondary; the decisive gap is **credibility at the moment of first contact**, because the buyer is a small-school operator who hears about SchoolMule through a personal email, checks the site on a phone, and trusts peers, plain facts and a visible price over polish. A site that says less and proves more (one named school, one honest hosting statement, one price, one real report card, one instant booking path) would already stand apart from a category that hides prices behind 10-field forms and shows stock photos instead of product.

The position is also narrower and stronger than "school management for everyone". MySchool owns "Canadian" for larger schools and Ad-Din owns Ontario Islamic schools, but nobody owns the small Ontario JK–8 school of any faith or method that has never had a real system and wants to see the price, the report card and the parent's phone before it talks to anyone. Claiming that space depends on decisions only the founder can make, chiefly the price, the proof statement and the security gaps, and those decisions are worth making before a single section is redesigned.
